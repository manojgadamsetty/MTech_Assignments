1. Executive Summary, Case Study and Scope

Civil construction projects frequently experience material wastage, theft, stockouts, manual data-entry errors and procurement delays. These issues are intensified across multiple construction sites where connectivity may be intermittent and material records are maintained on paper.

The Smart Construction Site Material & Inventory Management System digitizes material requests, purchase orders, delivery verification, inventory updates and reorder decisions in one auditable workflow. It is designed for site engineers and managers who need reliable field information even when the network is temporarily unavailable.

Case-study objective: Replace fragmented paper-based material tracking with a secure, auditable and offline-capable inventory workflow.
Target IndicatorTarget
Material wastage and theft20% reduction within six months
Stockout-induced work stoppages35% reduction
Purchase-order approval to dispatchLess than four hours
Physical-to-digital inventory accuracy98% match

1.1 In-Scope Functions

  • Material requisition and purchase-order management.
  • Supplier and purchase-order status tracking.
  • QR-code delivery-docket verification.
  • Recording of delivered quantity, batch number and storage yard.
  • Inventory balance updates and audit logging.
  • Low-stock detection, reorder recommendations and alerts.
  • Offline transaction capture using encrypted SQLite storage.
  • Automatic synchronization after network restoration.
  • Photo evidence for weighbridge slips and quality documents.
  • Role-based access for Site Engineers, Site Managers, Suppliers and Project Directors.

1.2 Out-of-Scope Functions

  • Payroll and employee attendance management.
  • Complete accounting and financial-ledger management.
  • Construction design and structural analysis.
  • Fleet maintenance and vehicle tracking.
  • Supplier payment processing.
  • Automated physical movement of material inside the site.

1.3 Assumptions

  • Each construction site has a unique site identifier.
  • Materials have configured minimum-stock and reorder quantities.
  • Approved suppliers provide valid purchase-order information.
  • Site Engineers have mobile devices capable of QR scanning.
  • Site Managers are authorized to approve purchase orders.

5. Significant Use-Case Specifications

5.1 UC-02: Log Material Delivery

Template FieldSpecification
GoalRecord and verify a material delivery and update site inventory.
Primary ActorSite Engineer / Gatekeeper.
Scope and LevelSmart Construction Inventory System; user-goal level.
TriggerA supplier shipment arrives at the construction-site gate.
PreconditionsUser is authenticated; purchase order exists in Dispatched state or local cache.
Main Success Scenario1. Select Receive Delivery. 2. Scan QR docket or enter PO ID. 3. Display PO details. 4. Enter quantity, batch and yard. 5. Attach photo. 6. Validate, create receipt, update inventory and mark PO Delivered.
Alternate FlowsPartial delivery changes PO to Partially Delivered. A quality defect creates a rejection slip and blocks stock addition.
Exception FlowIf offline, save the encrypted transaction locally and synchronize after reconnection.
PostconditionsInventory balance and DeliveryReceipt are updated; the audit log stores user, time and GPS.
Business RulesDelivered quantity cannot exceed 105% of PO quantity without manager approval. Weighbridge photo is mandatory for aggregate deliveries above five metric tons.
Special RequirementsQR scanning, offline operation, photo evidence, NFR-03 synchronization and NFR-04 security.

5.2 UC-04: Trigger Reorder and Process Purchase Order

Template FieldSpecification
GoalCreate and process a purchase order when available stock reaches the safety threshold.
Primary ActorStock Scheduler / Site Manager.
Scope and LevelSmart Construction Inventory System; system-goal level.
TriggerInventory verification detects stock less than or equal to the minimum threshold.
PreconditionsSafety stock and reorder quantity are configured; an approved supplier catalogue exists.
Main Success Scenario1. Detect low stock. 2. Create draft PO. 3. Notify manager. 4. Manager verifies budget and approves. 5. Transmit PO to supplier. 6. Supplier acknowledges and dispatches.
Alternate FlowsManager adjusts quantity; Engineer requests a manual reorder because of an impending weather delay.
Exception FlowSupplier returns Capacity Exceeded; the system alerts the manager and suggests a secondary approved supplier.
PostconditionsPO is Approved or Dispatched and the approval audit record is stored.
Business RulesOrders above $50,000 require Site Manager and Project Director approval. Auto-reorder is suppressed when yard capacity is above 90%.
Special RequirementsDual approval, audit trail, supplier fallback and four-hour approval-to-dispatch target.

6. Software Architecture

A three-tier layered client-server architecture separates presentation, business logic and persistence concerns. This supports mobile field use, offline operation, maintainability and independent scaling.

LayerTechnologyResponsibilities
Presentation / ClientReact Native PWA, SQLiteQR scanning, high-contrast field UI and local transaction caching.
Application / MiddleNode.js REST API, Express, sync engineAuthentication, RBAC, business rules, reorder logic and synchronization.
Persistence / BackendPostgreSQL, Redis, AWS S3Domain entities, stock cache, audit records and photo evidence.

10. Conclusion

The Smart Construction Site Material & Inventory Management System provides a complete software-engineering blueprint for controlling construction materials from request through delivery and stock reconciliation. The use-case model defines responsibilities, MoSCoW prioritization aligns delivery with business value, and the two detailed specifications capture functional and non-functional requirements. The three-tier architecture supports offline field operation, while the class, activity, state and sequence models establish a traceable design. The operation mapping demonstrates that class operations are derived directly from class-sequence interactions.
